Getting started

Overview

The Holodeck Portal lets you deploy, reach and manage your own nested VMware Cloud Foundation (VCF) and vSphere Foundation (VVF) labs from a browser.

Behind the portal is VMware Holodeck, which builds complete nested VCF and VVF environments (ESX hosts, vCenter, NSX, SDDC Manager and more) on a shared vCenter. The portal runs Holodeck for you: you pick a configuration, choose the lab type and options, and the portal handles the networking, the deployment and the clean-up.

What you can do

Deploy

Choose a configuration, lab type, version and Holodeck options. Networking is assigned for you.

Reach

Open the lab's webtop, see every console and DNS record, and read the lab's passwords.

Control

Shut down, power on, suspend and resume your lab; extend its lease; add clusters or hosts.

Follow

Watch Holodeck's live output and answer its questions without leaving the page.

Quick start

  1. Sign in at your portal's address (for example https://holodeck.example.com). See Signing in.
  2. Click Deploy a lab in the left menu, pick a configuration, give the lab a name and choose a type and version. When the Checks panel says Ready to deploy, click Deploy lab. See Deploying a lab.
  3. Watch the deployment on the lab's Activity tab. A full VCF deployment takes several hours. If the lab card shows Answer prompt, Holodeck needs input from you: see Answering prompts.
  4. When the lab is Ready, click Webtop on its card, sign in with the login shown under Credentials (if your administrator uses the webtop gateway, it signs you in by itself), and use Firefox inside the webtop to open the lab's consoles. See Accessing your lab.
  5. Shut the lab down when you're not using it and remove it when you're finished.

Getting started

Signing in

Sign in with a portal account or with your organisation's single sign-on, depending on how your administrator has set the portal up.

Sign-in options

The sign-in page shows the options your administrator has enabled:

  • Single sign-on buttons (OpenID Connect or SAML), for example Single Sign-On. You sign in at your identity provider and come back to the portal. Your role (user or administrator) comes from your groups there.
  • Username and password for portal (local) accounts. Your administrator creates these.

If neither works, ask your administrator to check that your account exists, is enabled, and (for single sign-on) that you are in a group allowed to use the portal.

Too many attemptsAfter 10 failed sign-ins for the same username from the same address within 15 minutes, the portal refuses further attempts for that username from that address for the rest of the period, even with the right password. One address is also allowed 50 failed sign-ins in 15 minutes across all usernames. Successful sign-ins don't count.
Single sign-on starts at the portalUse the single sign-on button on the portal's sign-in page (or an identity-provider tile that opens it). Responses that the identity provider sends without the portal having asked are refused. If you see "Sign-in session expired or was not started here", start again from the portal's sign-in page.

Sessions

A sign-in lasts 12 hours by default, after which you're asked to sign in again. Signing out (user menu at the bottom left → Sign out) ends the session immediately. If an administrator disables your account, you're signed out at once.

Your Account page (user menu → Account) lists every browser where you're signed in, with its address and when it signed in; "This browser" marks the one you're using. Sign out other sessions ends all the others at once, for example after using a shared computer or if you think someone else has your session.

Changing your password

Portal accounts only: open the user menu (your initials at the bottom of the left menu) → Account. Enter the current password and the new one twice; it must be at least 10 characters. Changing your password signs out every other browser where you're signed in; the one you're using stays signed in. Single sign-on users change their password at their identity provider.

Deploying a lab

The Deploy a lab page walks through four steps, checks everything as you go, and shows the exact Holodeck command that will run.

Open Deploy a lab from the left menu (or the Deploy a new lab tile on the dashboard). If you see "No configurations available", your administrator hasn't published a configuration to you yet.

1 · Configuration

Pick one of the configurations you may use. Each card shows its target vCenter, cluster and datastore. Some configurations pre-select a lab type, version and options for you; you can still change them. A configuration greyed out with all Holorouters in use has no free Holorouter right now; try again later or ask an administrator.

Deploying

Click Deploy lab. The portal reserves the network slot and Holorouter, queues the deployment and opens the lab's Activity tab with the live output. The lab is Queued until the job starts, then Deploying.

How long does it take?From under an hour for a small VVF lab to many hours for a VCF full stack. You can close the browser: the deployment continues and you get a notification when it finishes.
VCF 9.1 with an online depotHolodeck needs an activation code generated for the VCF Installer's Download Service ID. When the deployment reaches that point, the job asks you for it in the portal. See Answering prompts.
Working with labs

Answering prompts

Some Holodeck steps ask a question. The portal shows the question in the page and passes your answer to Holodeck.

How you'll know

  • The lab card's CURRENT line says "… — waiting for your input" and its footer shows an amber Answer prompt button.
  • The lab's Activity tab has an amber dot, and the job's status is Input required.
  • The dashboard's Jobs running tile turns red with "1 waiting for input".

Answering

  1. Click Answer prompt (or open the lab's Activity tab).
  2. Read the job output above the amber prompt bar; it usually explains what Holodeck needs (for VCF 9.1, the Download Service ID appears there).
  3. Type the answer and click Send, or use Yes / No for yes/no questions.

Secret answers (when Holodeck asks for a password or code with hidden input) are typed into a masked field. Answers are passed to Holodeck and not stored; the audit log records only that a prompt was answered.

The VCF 9.1 activation code

For VCF 9.1 with an online depot, Holodeck prints the VCF Installer's Download Service ID and waits for an activation code. Generate the code for that ID on the Broadcom support portal, then paste it into the prompt bar.

Answered automaticallySome questions, such as the confirmation when you remove a lab, are answered by the portal itself. The log then shows "(answered automatically by the portal)".

If you don't answer

A job waiting for an answer keeps its lab busy. By default you get a reminder notification after 24 hours, and the job is cancelled after 72 hours without an answer (your administrator can change both). A cancelled deployment leaves the lab Failed, so you can retry or remove it.

Working with labs

Accessing your lab

You reach a lab through its Holorouter's webtop: a desktop in your browser that sits inside the lab's network.

Lab networks and lab hostnames such as vc-mgmt-a.site-a.lab03.vcf.lab are only reachable from the lab's Holorouter. The webtop gives you a desktop on that Holorouter, with Firefox and the lab's DNS.

Opening the webtop

  1. On the lab card click Webtop, or on the lab's page open the Access tab and click Open webtop. It opens in a new browser tab.
  2. If the address is the portal's own, like https://holodeck.example.com:8443/, the webtop opens through the portal: it signs you in by itself and only you (the lab's owner) and administrators can open it. Otherwise (an address like http://192.0.2.11:30000/) sign in with the webtop login at the top of the lab's Credentials dialog ("Lab access — webtop on <router>").
  3. On the Access tab, click a console to copy its address, then paste it into Firefox inside the webtop.
Shared webtop loginEvery Holorouter's webtop uses the same login behind the scenes. Don't change settings in the webtop that would affect other users. If you hand a lab to someone else or remove it, your webtop session on its router ends.

Credentials

Click Credentials on the lab card menu or the lab's page. The dialog lists:

  • Lab access: the webtop address, user and password, with copy buttons. Through the portal no password is shown, because none is needed.
  • Lab passwords by component and account, for example vCenter administrator@vsphere.local, ESX hosts root, NSX Manager admin, SDDC Manager admin@local, VCF Installer or Cloud Builder accounts.

Passwords are hidden until you click Reveal passwords; the copy buttons work either way. Viewing credentials is recorded in the audit log. If the list is empty, run Refresh on the lab.

Provision-only labsIf the lab was deployed with Provision only, Holodeck built the hosts and appliances and you finished the installation yourself. The dialog then says so: the passwords you chose during the installation aren't known to Holodeck, so the listed ones match only where you entered the same passwords.

Refresh and diagnostics

Refresh asks Holodeck to re-read the lab (Sync-HoloDeckInstance), then updates the nested VM list, networks, DNS records, credentials and power state. Use it when something looks out of date.

Export diagnostics (failed labs) runs Holodeck's Export-ErrorDiagnostics with the full error stack. The output is in the job log, for your administrator.

Working with labs

Power and suspend

Shut a lab down when you aren't using it, suspend it to pick up exactly where you left off, and power it on or resume it when you return.

ActionAvailable whenWhat happens
Shut downThe lab isn't already off or suspendedHolodeck shuts down the workload domain, then the management domain, then powers off the nested ESX hosts. The portal then shuts down the VCF Installer / Cloud Builder VM (forcing it off after 5 minutes if needed). Takes several minutes.
Power onThe lab isn't fully onHolodeck starts the lab, then the portal powers on the installer VM.
SuspendThe lab is on or partially onAll of the lab's VMs are suspended together, so everything inside resumes where it left off.
ResumeThe lab is suspendedThe normal power-on, which resumes the suspended VMs.

Before you suspend

Suspending needs datastore spacevSphere writes each VM's memory to its datastore, roughly the lab's total configured memory (for example around 770 GB for a VCF 9.1 management domain). The job checks there is room first and stops with a message if there isn't; shut the lab down instead in that case.
  • After resuming, expect clock catch-up and a few alarms inside the lab.
  • A suspended lab can't be shut down directly: resume it first.

Following power jobs

While a power job runs, the power state shows Stopping…, Starting… or Suspending…, and the output is on the lab's Activity tab. When it finishes, the portal reads the real state of the VMs: Powered on, Powered off, Suspended or Partially on.

Expired leasesWhen a lease ends, the portal shuts the lab down for you. You can't power it on again until an administrator renews the lease.
Working with labs

Leases

Every lab has a lease. You're warned twice before it ends; when it ends the lab is powered off, never deleted.

Lease timeline

  1. Deploy: the lease starts with the days you chose (your administrator sets the default and the maximum).
  2. First warning: a notification "<lab>: lease ends in …" at the first lead time (by default one day before). The lease bar turns amber.
  3. Second warning: another notification at the second lead time (by default two hours before). The bar turns red.
  4. Expiry: the lab is marked expired and powered off. You and the administrators are notified. The lab and its data stay; an administrator can renew or remove it.

Extending a lease

Click Extend lease on the card menu or the lab's page. Pick a preset (+1d, +3d, +7d, +14d, +30d, depending on the maximum) or type the days to add, then click Extend. Extending resets the warnings.

  • The new end time can't be more than the maximum lease from now; the dialog shows the limit.
  • If your administrator has turned off lease extensions, the dialog says so; ask an administrator.
  • Once a lease has expired, only an administrator can renew it.

Idle labs

Your administrator may have labs that nobody uses suspended, or shut down, after some days. If so, the lab page shows how long no use has been seen and when the lab will be suspended or shut down, with a Keep running button, and you get a notification before each step.

  • Use counts: opening the lab's page, viewing its credentials, starting a power or day-2 action, extending the lease, Keep running, and working in its webtop.
  • A suspended lab comes back with Resume; a shut-down lab with Power on. Nothing is deleted.

Day-2 operations

Change a running lab with Holodeck's Update-HoloDeckInstance: add clusters, hosts, Supervisor or VCF Automation.

Open the lab's Day-2 tab (the lab must be Ready), click Configure… on an action, fill in the form and click Run. The job's output appears on the Activity tab and you get a notification when it completes or fails.

Host limits

Your administrator sets what Add ESX hosts may request. By default: 2–64 vCPUs and 8–1024 GB of memory per host, and 1–3 disks of 1–16384 GB each. The form shows the allowed range under each field and refuses values outside it.

A lab's site may also hold only so many nested hosts, 8 by default. A new lab has Holodeck's 4, every added cluster adds 3 and Add ESX hosts adds the number you ask for. So with the default limit you can add one cluster and one more host, or four single hosts. The Add cluster and Add ESX hosts dialogs show how many hosts the site has and how many more are allowed. Ask an administrator if you need more.

If adding hosts or a cluster fails or is cancelled, some of the new hosts may already exist. They keep counting, the portal refreshes the lab by itself to see which hosts are really there, and you can add hosts again once that refresh has finished.

Supervisor and VCF AutomationThese actions use Holodeck's shared 10.100.x routed networks, and the portal allows only one lab at a time to use them through day-2 operations. If another lab already uses them, the action is refused with that lab's name.
Working with labs

Activity and jobs

Every operation is a job with live output. The Activity page lists yours.

The Activity list

Columns: Operation (Deploy, Power, Refresh, Day-2, Diagnostics, Remove…), with details such as the power action or version; Lab / target; Status; Started; Duration; By. Click an operation to open its full-page console.

Statuses: Queued Running Input required Succeeded Failed Cancelled

The job console

  • Shows Holodeck's output with its colours. Each step starts with a ==> heading and the command that ran (passwords shown as ********).
  • Follow keeps the view at the newest line; scrolling up turns it off.
  • "Waiting for the Holodeck lock…" means the job is queued behind another job on the same Holorouter.
  • A failed job ends with Job failed: <reason>.

Cancelling a job

Click Cancel in the console and confirm. Holodeck is stopped mid-operation. A cancelled deployment marks the lab Failed; you can retry it (Holodeck resumes from where it was) or remove the lab.

Working with labs

Network pool

Each lab gets its own addresses and VLANs automatically, from a pool your administrator defines.

The pool is divided into slots. Each slot belongs to one Holorouter and holds one lab. A slot reserves two /20 networks: site A is deployed, site B is held for future dual-site labs. VLAN ranges start at the network's second octet × 100.

SlotSite ASite A VLANsSite B (reserved)Site B VLANs

210.3.0.0/20300–33110.4.0.0/20400–431

Example with the default pool (10.1 – 10.10, five slots). Your portal's pool may differ; the Network pool page shows the real one.

On the Network pool page each slot shows your lab's name if it's yours, In use for someone else's lab, Available when its Holorouter is free, or No Holorouter when the slot has no router yet. Removing a lab returns its slot.

Each lab also gets its own DNS domain, normally lab plus its two-digit slot: lab03.vcf.lab on slot 3.

Working with labs

Retry and remove

Retry a failed deployment where it stopped, or remove a lab to delete its VMs and free its slot.

Retrying a failed deployment

When a deployment fails, read the end of the log (View log on the card). If the cause was temporary, or has been fixed, click Retry deployment. Holodeck resumes from its saved state instead of starting over.

Removing a lab

  1. Click Remove lab (card menu) or Remove (lab page).
  2. Type the lab's instance ID to confirm and click Remove lab.
  3. The portal runs Holodeck's removal, deletes any VMs Holodeck left behind (such as the installer VM), checks that nothing remains, and frees the configuration.

When it finishes the lab is Removed, its passwords are deleted from the portal, and you get a notification that its network slot is free again. This can't be undone.

If removal failsThe lab goes to Failed with "Removal failed: …". Read the log; you can run Remove lab again, or ask an administrator.

Current limits

What the portal doesn't do yet, so you can plan around it.

  • One lab per Holorouter. Holodeck configures a Holorouter's networks, routing and DNS for one lab, so each lab gets its own Holorouter and network slot. Capacity grows as administrators add Holorouters.
  • Access is through the webtop. Lab networks aren't routed to your VPN or desktop; use the lab's webtop.
  • Every Holorouter's webtop uses the same login, over plain HTTP, unless your administrator opens webtops through the portal.
  • No email. Notifications appear in the portal's bell only.
  • Nested hosts per lab site are limited (8 by default, including the lab's own 4).
  • Single-site labs only. Each lab's site B networks are reserved for future dual-site support.
  • Supervisor and VCF Automation day-2 actions run on one lab at a time.
  • Versions depend on the installation files your administrator has staged; not every version may be available on every Holorouter.
